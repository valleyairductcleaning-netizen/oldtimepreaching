01 / The story

A business built on work, not just an idea.

My name is Tim. I work on vehicles in the Cornwall, Ontario area, using years of hands-on experience to repair problems and help customers keep their vehicles on the road.

I am not seeking support to begin something I have never tried. I am already doing the work. But building a stable business requires more than mechanical ability: it requires appropriate workspace, operating capital, equipment, and enough financial stability to continue serving customers while the business grows.

My faith matters to how I approach this work. I want to be truthful about the condition of a vehicle, fair in my dealings, and diligent in the quality of the repair.

“The goal is not to live on support. The goal is to build a business that can provide for my family through honest work.”

04 / The path forward

From working today to standing independently.

Now: keep serving customers

Continue actual paid repair work, document completed jobs, and build a reliable record of revenue and expenses.

Next: strengthen the foundation

Secure a suitable legal workspace and critical equipment as funding and business demand justify.

Then: sustain the household

Grow earned income so ordinary business revenue—not ongoing gifts—can cover operations and household living costs.

05 / The convictions behind the work

Old preaching. Practical faith.

Charles H. Spurgeon's sermon “Robbers of God”, preached in 1890 on Malachi 3:8, challenges readers to think seriously about stewardship and generosity. It is one reason I believe Christian convictions should produce practical action.

At the same time, supporting this business is a voluntary decision. Giving to a private business is not a biblical requirement, and no one should be made to feel spiritually guilty for choosing not to contribute.
