GTA GTA6: Should Christians Play Grand Theft Auto VI? A Biblical Rebuke

Can a professing Christian glorify God while enjoying a game filled with violence, sexual immorality, and rebellion?

Old Time Preaching · November 19, 2026 · Christian Living

Can you honestly imagine Jesus Christ sitting down to play Grand Theft Auto 6?

That is a question I would like every professing Christian who plays GTA 6 to seriously consider.

With the release of Grand Theft Auto VI, millions of people are excited to spend countless hours exploring a fictional world of crime, violence, sexual immorality, and rebellion.

But what concerns me is not simply that the world enjoys such things. The world has always loved its sin. What concerns me is that many professing Christians see absolutely nothing wrong with entertaining themselves with the very things God condemns.

And when confronted, what is often their response? “It's just a game!”

Really? Is that the best defence we can offer? Are we going to excuse everything we put before our eyes simply because it is presented as entertainment?

I believe the problem goes much deeper than video games. It reveals something about the condition of our hearts, the influence of worldly culture upon the church, and how lightly we have begun to treat sin. Let us examine these things in light of Scripture.

1. GTA 6 Promotes the Very Things God Condemns

Before debating whether Christians should play GTA 6, consider what kind of entertainment we are talking about. The Entertainment Software Rating Board rates Grand Theft Auto VI “Mature 17+.” Its listed content warnings include blood and gore, intense violence, mature humour, nudity, strong language, strong sexual content, and use of drugs and alcohol. These are not imagined objections; they come from the game's official rating summary.

Murder is sin. Sexual immorality is sin. Filthy speech is sin. Taking pleasure in wickedness is no small matter before a holy God. Yet professing Christians purchase entertainment filled with these things and defend their right to enjoy it.

Have we become so comfortable with sin that we no longer recognize how offensive it is to God?

2. “It's Just a Game!” — But What Is in Your Heart?

Someone says, “I'm not actually murdering anyone. I'm not really stealing anything. I'm only controlling a character in a video game.” Certainly, playing a fictional game is not the same outward act as committing an actual murder. But does that settle the moral question? Absolutely not.

Jesus taught that sin is not merely a matter of outward actions. In Matthew 5:27–28, He explained that a man can be guilty of adultery in his heart through lustful intent even without committing the physical act. The heart matters to God.

When you choose entertainment that lets you act out wickedness in a fictional world, you cannot dismiss every question about your motives by saying none of it is real. Why do you enjoy it? Are you observing evil as part of a story, or enjoying the opportunity to participate in it?

I am not saying every player has literally committed each sin shown on the screen. I am saying that we cannot hide behind “it's just a game” to avoid examining our hearts. God is concerned not only with what our hands are doing but also with what our hearts are loving.

Can you honestly picture Jesus Christ enjoying this game?

3. Romans 1:32 — Taking Pleasure in Wickedness

Romans 1 describes humanity's rebellion against God. By the end of the chapter, Paul speaks not only about those who practise wickedness but also those who approve of it (Romans 1:32). Paul is describing actual moral rebellion, not writing specifically about modern video games. Still, his warning about approving of wickedness should trouble anyone who finds pleasure in sin.

Do we really believe a holy God is unconcerned when His people deliberately seek entertainment that celebrates what He condemns?

If you believe GTA 6 is acceptable entertainment for a follower of Jesus Christ, here is my challenge: Show me from Scripture how playing GTA 6 glorifies God. Do not simply tell me you enjoy the game, that everyone else plays it, or that it is fictional. Open your Bible and defend your position.

“Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.” — 1 Corinthians 10:31 (ASV)

That includes our entertainment.

4. What Are You Putting Before Your Eyes?

“I will set no base thing before mine eyes.” — Psalm 101:3 (ASV)

Professing Christians can spend hours watching vulgarity, sexual immorality, and cruelty, then defend it as though Scripture has nothing to say about their enjoyment. Philippians 4:8 calls believers to dwell on what is true, honourable, just, pure, lovely, and worthy of praise. Ephesians 5:11 tells us to have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather to expose them.

There is a difference between learning about evil and celebrating evil. A historical war game might portray the horrors of battle without glorifying cruelty. A story about murder might expose sin's consequences rather than invite us to enjoy wickedness. Each game must be evaluated according to its content and purpose.

But when the attraction of entertainment is enjoying wicked behaviour, Christians ought to recognize the danger. We should not be searching for the closest we can get to sin. We should be pursuing holiness.

5. Christian Parents, What Are You Allowing Into Your Homes?

I want to speak directly to Christian parents. What are you allowing your children to play? Have you examined the games they are purchasing, or have you accepted that because something is popular, it must be harmless?

Some parents respond, “My children know the difference between a game and reality.” But that answer misses the point.

“Be not deceived: Evil companionships corrupt good morals.” — 1 Corinthians 15:33 (ASV)

A video game is not literally a human companion. Nevertheless, the influences we willingly embrace matter. Just as we would be concerned about our children continually surrounding themselves with wicked company, we should be concerned about the entertainment they repeatedly consume.

Ephesians 6:4 commands fathers to bring up their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. Parents, you cannot teach your children that murder and sexual immorality are wrong while carelessly providing entertainment that treats these things as amusement.

You are not simply raising children to be entertained. You are responsible for teaching them to fear and honour God.

6. We Are Wasting the Little Time God Has Given Us

There is another issue that deserves far more attention: time. This is not merely about GTA 6. We waste hours on YouTube, social media, television, and countless other distractions. Some are not inherently sinful, but are we being wise stewards of the time God has entrusted to us?

Ephesians 5:15–16 calls us to walk wisely, redeeming the time because the days are evil. How easily can someone play games for hours while struggling to find fifteen minutes to read the Bible? Where is our time for prayer, our families, serving others, and proclaiming the Gospel?

James 4:14 compares our lives to a vapour that appears for a little time and vanishes. Romans 14:12 says each of us shall give account of himself to God.

I am not arguing that Christians can never rest or enjoy recreation. I am asking whether entertainment has become more important to us than the God who gave us life.

We have only one life to live. Why are we so willing to waste it?

7. The Culture of the World Has Infected the Church

This problem is bigger than Grand Theft Auto. I believe the culture of the world has infected the culture of the church. Rather than examining the world through the lens of Scripture, too many Christians examine Scripture through the preferences of the world.

Romans 12:2 tells us not to be conformed to this world but to be transformed by the renewing of our minds. Where are the pastors confronting worldly entertainment, holiness, repentance, and the condition of the heart?

Could part of the problem be that some pastors are themselves caught up in the same distractions? Pastors are sinners too; they are not immune from worldly temptations. But they are called to faithfully preach God's Word.

“Preach the word; be urgent in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort, with all longsuffering and teaching.” — 2 Timothy 4:2 (ASV)

The responsibility of the pastor is not to make people comfortable in their sin. The church does not need to become more like the world. The church needs to return to Scripture.

8. Professing Christian, Examine Yourself

If you profess to follow Jesus Christ but see nothing wrong with enjoying games built around wickedness, have you examined your heart? Are you sincerely seeking to honour Christ, or merely searching for ways to defend the pleasures of this world?

I am not saying that playing GTA 6 automatically proves someone is not saved. Salvation is not earned by avoiding video games. But genuine saving faith produces a changed relationship with sin. A Christian can struggle, stumble, and need correction. A person who loves wickedness without conviction, however, has reason for serious self-examination.

Second Corinthians 13:5 commands us to examine ourselves to see whether we are in the faith. If this article makes you uncomfortable, do not immediately become defensive. Open your Bible. Ask God to search your heart.

The question is not whether you can find another Christian who agrees with you. The question is whether your choices please God.

9. A Warning to Those Who Do Not Know Christ

Perhaps you are reading this and are not a Christian. You may wonder why anyone is concerned about a video game. But the real issue is greater than GTA 6. The real issue is sin.

Romans 3:23 teaches that all have sinned and fall short of God's glory. That includes you, and it includes me. We are not sinners merely because we have watched or played the wrong things. We are sinners by nature, and our sinful hearts produce sinful thoughts, desires, words, and actions.

God is holy and righteous, and He will judge sin. Romans 6:23 says the wages of sin is death. Yet there is good news: God has provided salvation through His Son, Jesus Christ.

Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of God, took on human flesh, lived a perfectly righteous life, and willingly went to the cross. He died for sinners, bearing the punishment their sins deserved. He was buried and rose bodily from the dead on the third day.

Salvation is not earned through becoming a better person. It is God's gift of grace, received through faith in Jesus Christ. The answer is not merely to throw away a game and try to become more moral.

You need a new heart. You need forgiveness. You need Jesus Christ.

“Repent ye, and believe in the gospel.” — Mark 1:15 (ASV)

Turn from your sin and trust in Jesus Christ. Do not delay. You do not know how much time you have left.

Conclusion: Can You Play GTA 6 to the Glory of God?

Can you honestly imagine Jesus Christ sitting down to enjoy Grand Theft Auto 6? Can you picture our holy and sinless Saviour taking pleasure in wickedness?

Not every game containing violence is automatically sinful; portraying evil is different from glorifying it. But GTA 6 raises serious questions about the enjoyment of criminality, sexual immorality, vulgarity, and violence. I believe the biblical principles examined here give Christians compelling reasons to reject this kind of entertainment.

Our entertainment choices are not separate from our walk with Christ. Our hearts, minds, bodies, and time belong to Him.

If you disagree, examine these claims in the light of Scripture. But please do not simply tell me, “It's just a game.” Show me from the Bible how taking pleasure in this kind of entertainment glorifies Jesus Christ.

Life is short, eternity is real, and every one of us will give an account before God. May we live for the glory of Jesus Christ rather than defend the pleasures of a world that is passing away.

“For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.” — Philippians 1:21 (ASV)
