Should Christians Play GTA 6? A Biblical Guide to Discernment

What the official game rating and Scripture can—and cannot—tell Christians weighing Grand Theft Auto VI.

Old Time Preaching · November 19, 2026 · Christian Living

Should Christians play GTA 6? If you are asking whether GTA 6 is appropriate for Christians, the rating is a useful starting point, not the whole answer. The Bible does not name modern video games, so Christians should not pretend that one verse gives a simple, direct ruling. But Scripture does teach us to examine what we enjoy, what shapes our minds, and whether our choices honour God. The official rating for Grand Theft Auto VI gives families concrete information to consider; it does not make the conscience decision for every person.

This is a biblical discernment article, not a firsthand review of the gameplay. The official ESRB rating and content descriptors for GTA VI are the source for the content details discussed here.

So, should Christians play GTA 6?

What does the official GTA 6 rating say?

The ESRB rates Grand Theft Auto VI “Mature 17+.” Its listed content descriptors include blood and gore, intense violence, mature humour, nudity, strong language, strong sexual content, and use of drugs and alcohol. The rating also notes in-game purchases.

That is more useful than relying on rumours or a vague description such as “an action game.” Parents and players can read the full ESRB rating summary for themselves. A rating describes content; it does not settle every moral question, explain how a particular player will respond, or replace parental judgment.

What does the Bible teach about entertainment?

First Corinthians 10:31 gives a broad principle: “Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God” (ASV). Entertainment is part of life, not a spiritual exception. Christians can ask whether a choice honours God, serves what is good, and leaves room for the responsibilities He has given them.

Philippians 4:8 calls believers to dwell on what is true, honourable, just, pure, lovely, and worthy of praise. That does not mean every story or work of art must be cheerful or explicitly Christian. Scripture itself records terrible sins. The important questions include how evil is portrayed and whether we are being invited to understand its consequences—or to take pleasure in it.

So “it is only a game” does not end the discussion. Nor does the presence of fictional wrongdoing, by itself, prove that every player is committing that wrongdoing. We need to consider the content, its presentation, our reasons for choosing it, and its effect on us.

Is playing fictional crime the same as committing sin?

No. Controlling a fictional character is not automatically the same as committing a real crime. Christians should be careful not to make accusations that Scripture does not make. A story can depict evil in order to expose it, warn against it, or show its consequences.

At the same time, Jesus teaches that the heart matters, not only outward actions. When considering a game built around criminal activity and mature content, a Christian can honestly ask: What am I being asked to enjoy here? Is the story making sin's destructiveness clear, or am I seeking entertainment from the very attitudes and acts God warns against?

Different people may answer that question differently, but sincerity matters. We should not use the fact that an action is fictional to dismiss every concern about desire, conscience, and what we are training ourselves to enjoy.

Can a game affect a Christian's conscience?

Repeated exposure can influence what feels normal. That does not mean every person who plays a violent game will become violent, and this article makes no such claim. The more personal question is whether particular entertainment is dulling your concern about sin, feeding temptation, or troubling your conscience.

Paul's words in 1 Corinthians 6:12 are helpful: “All things are lawful for me; but not all things are expedient.” Christian liberty is not merely permission to do anything not named in a rule. We can also ask whether a choice is beneficial, whether it is mastering us, and whether we can make it with a clear conscience before God.

What about Christian parents and GTA 6?

The ESRB's “Mature 17+” rating and its specific content descriptors give parents a strong reason to review the game carefully rather than assuming it is suitable because it is popular. Ephesians 6:4 calls parents to raise their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. Guidance about games and media is one part of that responsibility.

Parents can read the rating summary, consider their child's age and maturity, learn what the game involves, and talk openly about why a boundary is being set. Popularity is not a reason to hand over parental judgment. Every household has to make its decision responsibly; no child's situation can be assessed from an article alone.

Questions Christians can ask before playing

  • What does the official rating say about this game's content?
  • Why do I want to play it, and what parts am I looking forward to?
  • Does it tempt me, trouble my conscience, or make sin seem trivial?
  • Is the time I spend on it interfering with worship, relationships, work, or other duties?
  • Could I explain my choice honestly before God and to a trusted Christian?
  • If I am a parent, have I reviewed the content and considered my child's maturity?

These are not a checklist for judging another person's salvation. They are prompts for examining our own choices with humility and honesty.

So, should Christians play GTA 6?

A Christian is not saved or lost based on a list of approved entertainment. Salvation is by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. But that does not make our choices unimportant. The Bible calls believers to holiness, a renewed mind, self-control, and love for what is good.

Given the official content rating for Grand Theft Auto VI, I believe Christians have serious reasons to avoid the game. Its combination of intense violence, nudity, strong sexual content, strong language, and drugs and alcohol is not a small concern. Other Christians may weigh the question differently; no one should pretend that the Bible names GTA 6 specifically or claim that every player has the same motive.

The more useful question than “Can I get away with playing it?” is whether this is wise and spiritually helpful for me—or appropriate for my child. If your conscience is unsettled, do not silence it simply because a game is popular. Seek wisdom, read the rating for yourself, and choose what you can do with a clear conscience before God.
